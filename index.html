Question 3: Solve for x: 3(x − 2) = 2x + 5

STRONG ANSWER
3(x − 2) = 2x + 5 3x − 6 = 2x + 5 3x − 2x = 5 + 6 x = 11 Check: 3(11 − 2) = 27 and 2(11) + 5 = 27 ✓
AI-generated feedback Excellent work! You distributed the 3 correctly, collected the x-terms on one side, and verified your solution by substituting it back. Your steps are clear and easy to follow. Try a challenge next: equations with fractions.
Teacher's Review The AI's feedback was accurate, but "challenge next" was too vague, so I named a specific next step (fractions). I also added praise for the check step, because that habit is what I want to reinforce.
AVERAGE ANSWER
3x − 6 = 2x + 5 x = 11
AI-generated feedback Correct answer! You distributed properly and found x = 11. To improve, show the middle steps you skipped and check your answer by substituting it back into the equation.
Teacher's Review The AI didn't notice that the student jumped from "3x − 6 = 2x + 5" to "x = 11". This may mean the student did the middle steps mentally, or copied the answer. I added a request to explain how they moved the terms, and I suggested a quick oral check in class.
WEAK ANSWER (COMMON MISTAKE)
3(x − 2) = 2x + 5 3x − 2 = 2x + 5 x = 7
AI-generated feedback You are on the right track with moving the x-terms together, but there is an error in the first line. When you multiply 3 by (x − 2), the 3 must multiply both terms, so 3 × (−2) = −6, not −2. Redo the problem with this correction.
Teacher's Review The AI correctly found the mistake (not distributing to the second term) but gave no hint about why it matters, and "redo the problem" is discouraging. I added a short worked example (2(x + 1) = 2x + 2) and a tip: "draw arrows from the outside number to every term inside the brackets."

3. Transparency & Prompts Used

Prompt 1: Generating the quiz

Act as an experienced Grade 9 mathematics teacher.
Create a 5-question multiple-choice quiz on Basic Algebra
(simplifying expressions, solving linear equations,
substitution, and factoring a difference of squares).

Requirements:
- Exactly 4 options per question, labeled A, B, C, D.
- Only one correct answer per question.
- Make wrong options based on common student mistakes.
- Vary the position of the correct answer.
- After each question, give the correct answer and a
  1-2 sentence explanation suitable for a 14-year-old.
- Use plain English and no more than moderate difficulty.
Return the result as a numbered list.

Prompt 2: Generating feedback

You are a supportive math teacher giving feedback.

Question: Solve for x: 3(x - 2) = 2x + 5
Correct answer: x = 11

Student answer:
"""
[PASTE STUDENT ANSWER HERE]
"""

Write feedback for a 14-year-old student in 3-4 sentences:
1. Start with one specific thing the student did well.
2. If there is a mistake, identify exactly where it
   happened and explain why it is incorrect.
3. Give one concrete next step to improve.
4. Use an encouraging, respectful tone.
Do not just give the final answer if the student is wrong.

4. Reflection

AI helped me most with speed. It drafted five algebra questions, plausible wrong options, and first-draft explanations, which gave me more time to think about quality. It also suggested the common mistake of forgetting to distribute, which shaped my weak sample answer.

However, AI was not always accurate or sufficient. Its feedback sometimes sounded generic, praised correct answers without noticing skipped steps, and assumed the student's reasoning instead of asking for it. I had to check every calculation and rewrite parts of the feedback so it was specific and encouraging.

Next time, I would give the AI a clearer student level, request feedback in short steps, test the quiz with real students, and keep the final judgment with the teacher.
